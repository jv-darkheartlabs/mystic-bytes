## Hook & thesis
Every Willingham heroine is a woman standing in a room she swore she’d never re-enter. Claire Campbell’s room is her whole hometown: the sister-shaped absence at its center, a mother whose memory is dissolving, and a farm up the road where the vines are trained, the beauty is maintained by hand, and a girl once went missing in the rows. **Verdict:** The most assured of the four — setting and structure in full balance at last; the cost is a suspect list more legible than her earlier books dared.

## The contextual pivot
The fourth novel completes a quiet arc across the shelf: the killer’s daughter, the victim’s mother, the chosen friend, and now the sister who left. Published in 2025, into a genre Willingham no longer borrows from but helps define, it returns to her first material — a cold case in a warm place — with the difference that this time the investigator is looking at her own family’s silence, not a stranger’s crime.

## Deep-dive critique
The vineyard is the book’s governing image: beauty as cultivation, rows trained up trellises, rot managed or hidden — a landscape that admits, in its own grammar, that lovely surfaces are labor and that some of the labor is burial. Against it runs the novel’s second investigation, inside Claire’s mother, whose dementia is dissolving the only archive the family kept. Willingham’s rationing structure is by now a signature, and here it’s cleanest: two disappearances, twenty-two years apart, each releasing facts that reprice the other, and the book plays fair with both timelines.

The relative cost is legibility. The farm’s circle of suspects sits closer to the surface than the buried households of *Flicker* or *Dangerous Things* — the whodunit frame shows its seams earlier. But the emotional math is the most exact she’s done: Claire has to decide, in the end, what remembering is *for* — evidence, or loyalty — and the novel refuses to pretend those are the same errand.

## Discussion launchpad
Natalie’s case went cold in a place where everyone knew her name. This is a book about forgetting as a community project — and the one woman who resigns from it.

**Questions for the room:**  
- What does this novel say small towns do with their missing girls — and who does the doing?  
- Claire’s mother is forgetting by illness while the town forgets by agreement. Whose forgetting does the book treat as mercy, and whose as crime?  
- The vineyard’s beauty is trained and maintained by hand. What in this story is trained up the trellis — and what is buried under it?  
- Chloe stayed, Isabelle stayed, Margot went toward. Claire is the one who left and came back. What does the leaving change about the investigating?  
- A forget-me-not is a promise disguised as a flower. Who in this novel keeps a promise by remembering — and who keeps one by letting go?
