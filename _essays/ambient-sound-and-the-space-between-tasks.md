Audio between tasks should mark a state change, not compete with the next block of work.

I used to leave a lyric-heavy mix running from a call straight into a mystic-bytes essay edit. The meeting ended. The mix did not. I would spend the first ten minutes of writing arguing with a chorus I was no longer choosing to hear. Silence, or low ambient sound without words, does one job: it tells the nervous system the previous mode is over. Meeting ended. Coding begins. That marker is cheap compared with the cost of a smeared switch.

They treat background audio as decoration. It is closer to a scheduler. A hard cut — pause, a few seconds of nothing, then a texture with no lyrics — beats a stream that never stops. Autoplay that surprises a housemate or a coworker is not ambience; it is an interrupt you inflicted on someone else's buffer. Headphones are a boundary device. They keep your marker from becoming their incident.

When the work wants pulse instead of texture, change the set. Do not ask one mix to do both jobs. Pair with [BPM and deep work](/p/how-bpm-affects-deep-work-focus/) when tempo is the input, not air.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
