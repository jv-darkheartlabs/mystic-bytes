Beats per minute is a cognitive dial. Choose it before you choose the task.

I keep three BPM-tagged sets for Dark Heart Labs work because one playlist cannot serve architecture and a hotfix. Below about 80 BPM, planning spreads out — the mystic-bytes outline pass, the Nightbind storage decision that should not be rushed. Between 80 and 120, I entrain into steady execution; that is where most of the coding happens. Above 120, urgency rises. Fine for a ten-minute burst. Poor for careful review. When a session feels forced, I check the tempo before I check my discipline.

Entrainment is not mysticism. Biological rhythms sync to an external pulse. They pick a "focus mix" by genre and then wonder why a 140 BPM set makes a design review feel like a chase. The mismatch is the input, not the person. Slow for design. Moderate for implementation. Fast only when the scope is tiny and reversible.

Maintain separate playlists or ambient sets tagged by range. Change tempo mid-session when friction appears. The playlist serves the work, not the other way around. This pairs with [games as systems thinking](/p/games-teach-systems-thinking/) — rhythm is a system input, same as a frame budget.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
