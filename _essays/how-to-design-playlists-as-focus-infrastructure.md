A playlist is a state machine for attention: warmup, hold, stop — not a shuffled bag of songs.

Random shuffle is chaos for deep work. I curate darkheartlabs session sets the same way I curate a stream overlay: opening track sets tempo, the middle maintains it, the close signals done. Cross-fade or a beat of silence between modes matters as much as genre. A lyric that demands language will compete with the sentence you are trying to write. Instrumental or low-lyric tracks reduce that fight. The same playlist, used on purpose, becomes a habit cue. The brain learns that this sound means ship mode.

They build one mega-mix and wonder why the cooldown never arrives. Encode intent in the list itself. Warmup is not the focus block. The last track is a stop bit, not an encore. Document what worked in a note file next to the playlist — BPM range, whether words survived, how long the hold lasted. I keep that note beside the darkheartlabs session lists so I do not relearn it every quarter. Playlists are living configs. Retire the ones that started pulling you into urgency you did not schedule.

Curate the transitions. Then press play on purpose.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
