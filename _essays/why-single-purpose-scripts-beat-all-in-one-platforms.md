Before you buy another platform seat, ask whether forty lines of script would close most of the job.

Platforms promise a suite. Scripts deliver one fix today. I keep a small lint helper in mystic-bytes that checks essay frontmatter and word bands. A vendor wanted to own "content ops." The script already failed the merge when a micro drifted under length. Composition still wins: clear stdin and stdout, versioned in the repo, owned by whoever can read a README.

Your aliases and one-off CLIs save minutes. Minutes compound into days across a year. Document them so they survive a laptop swap. They wait on procurement for a dashboard that will still need a human to click the same three steps the script already chains.

Small tools are how you keep leverage without a new contract. Do one thing. Pipe it. Repeat. If the job grows a second job, write a second script instead of growing a platform you do not control.

Start with the script. Buy the platform when the script has failed twice for a reason a vendor actually solves.
