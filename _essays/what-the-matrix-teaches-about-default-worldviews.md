The green grade was a default you were meant to stop noticing. Frameworks do the same with config. Defaults train perception until they look like laws.

I lost a mystic-bytes collection because inherited permalink and markdown settings dropped it without an error I recognized. The scaffold had been "fine" for months. Then a new folder did not match the pattern the generator assumed, and the pages simply did not exist. The Matrix's tint signals artificial reality before anyone explains it. Linter presets, org templates, and `jekyll new` output are worldviews you inherit. They are not neutral. They encode what the authors thought a site, a service, or a good file looked like. Question them before they go invisible.[^1]

Read your production config after a year. Note every assumption that baked in: timezone, permalink style, which files are drafts, which checks are warnings. If you cannot explain why a default is there, it is still a choice. It is just no longer yours.

Open the preset. Change the ones that are load-bearing. Leave a comment for the next reader who will treat it as physics.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.

[^1]: Richard H. Thaler and Cass R. Sunstein, *Nudge* (Yale University Press, 2008) — defaults are design, not absence of design; they steer behavior whether you named them or not.
