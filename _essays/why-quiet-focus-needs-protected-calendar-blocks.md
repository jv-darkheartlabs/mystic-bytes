Empty calendar space reads as availability. That is why it disappears.

Quiet focus needs a protected block the way a deploy freeze needs a title. I used to leave mornings open "in case something came up." Something always did. The block that survived was the one named honestly — deep work, not free — and treated by the team as occupied. Open calendars fill because empty looks polite. Polite is how you lose the only hours that can hold a hard bug.

They expect instant reply inside those hours and then wonder why estimates slip. Set an async SLA and keep it. A ping can wait forty-five minutes. A broken context cannot be refunded.

I protect the block the way I protect a freeze: visible, named, and boring to violate. Sensory load is a system resource; so is silence. If you do not schedule it, the calendar will schedule it for you as meetings. A debug session split across four "quick syncs" costs more than the meeting minutes suggest.

Title the hours. Hold the SLA. Treat a stolen focus block like a stolen deploy window.
