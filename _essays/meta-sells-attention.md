## Thesis

**Meta sells attention.** Facebook, Instagram, and the rest of the Family of Apps are inventory. Advertisers buy the inventory. Reality Labs is a long, expensive bet that the next inventory will be a headset. Platform policy is how the inventory stays sellable.

## Context

I have an Instagram account and a WhatsApp install because other people already do. I do not publish mystic-bytes into a Meta feed as a growth strategy. The writing lives on a site I host. I use the apps the way a remote person in Auckland uses them in 2026: messages, a few accounts I actually wanted, a lot of ranking I did not ask for. I have never worked at Meta. The useful documents are the 10-K, the Community Standards, and the public wreckage of the old Platform API.

Calling these products "communities" is a category error. Communities do not run a ranking function whose job is dwell time. Advertisers do not buy communities. They buy impressions next to whatever the ranker decided you would not close.

## Mechanism

**The feed is a ranking problem with an ads invoice.** Meta's annual report still describes a Family of Apps funded overwhelmingly by advertising.[^1] That is the business. The mechanism is a model that predicts what you will linger on, then inserts a paid slot. Reels, Stories, the old News Feed: different chrome, same job. Engagement is not a vibe. It is the objective function. When people say the apps "got worse," they often mean the ranker got better at its actual assignment. I have closed Instagram after twenty minutes and been unable to name a single post I meant to open. That is not a personal failing. That is a successful session from the seller's side.

**WhatsApp is the graph that does not look like a feed.** Messaging is utility. Utility is how you stay installed when the feed feels extractive. Meta bought WhatsApp in 2014 and spent years arguing it was a separate culture. Business accounts, status, channels, and the slow arrival of ads around the edges are how a messenger becomes inventory without admitting it in the first release notes. I keep WhatsApp because people in other timezones keep WhatsApp. That is a lock-in that does not need a like button.

**Reality Labs is future inventory, billed as research.** The segment loses money in public filings, year after year, at a scale that would kill a company that was only a headset shop. Meta can afford it because ads print the cash. Quest and the Horizon language are a bet that the next attention surface will be owned the way the phone feed is owned. I have not bought a Quest. I can still read a loss-making segment as strategy: if the phone ranker saturates, buy the face.

**Platform policy is the control surface.** Community Standards, ad policies, and API lockdown after Cambridge Analytica are not a civics class. They are how a company that sells attention survives regulators, brand safety teams, and its own history of letting third parties vacuum the social graph.[^2] When Instagram throttled the old API, developers called it a betrayal. It was a portfolio decision: the graph is too valuable to rent cheaply. I do not need a conspiracy to explain a closed API. I need an incentive. Attention you cannot meter is attention you cannot sell.

**You are not the customer unless you bought an ad.** Creator funds and "professional dashboards" complicate the sentence without reversing it. The advertiser is the customer. The user is the inventory with a login. I try to remember that when an app asks for more notifications. Notifications are a collection mechanism.

## Tradeoffs

**Reach vs being the product.** If you need to find a person who only lives on Instagram, you will open Instagram. That is rational. It is also how the ranker gets another session. Prefer a link to your own site. Use the app as a pointer, not as a CMS.

**Messenger utility vs family gravity.** WhatsApp is the hardest to leave because it is the address book. Leaving Facebook is easy if you never posted. Leaving the messenger is a group project. Budget the group project, or admit you are staying for other people's defaults.

**When the apps are the right tool.** Event coordination, a family chat, a business that already bought the ads: use them as tools. Turn off the feed if you can. I treat Meta like a casino I enter for a specific table. I do not owe the floor a wander.

## Close

Attention is the SKU. Ads are the payment. Policy is the fence. Reality Labs is the sequel if the phone saturates. I will keep the messenger I cannot replace and keep mystic-bytes off the ranker. Call the company by what it sells. It sells you, in slices, to people with a media budget.
