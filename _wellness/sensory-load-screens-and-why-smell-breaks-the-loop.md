After four hours of screens, my eyes are not the only thing tired. The visual loop keeps asking for another tab. Slack, the editor, a docs comment, the brightness that never quite matches the room. ADHD makes that loop cheaper to stay in than to leave. I do not need a lecture about willpower. I need a channel that is not another pixel.

Smell is a different input. It does not compete for the same attention as a notification badge. That is anatomy and habit, not a biohack. I am not optimizing VO2 or tricking a nervous system into a productivity skin. I am marking a state change when the display has used up the hour. A two-minute reset: stand, water, window if I have one, one scent I already paired with "we are stopping" or "we are starting." Then sit down or walk away. The timer matters more than the oil.

I keep this small on purpose. A personal inhaler or a short diffuser session in a room I control. Not a fog that fills a shared office. Strong smell can raise load for me and for anyone nearby, especially on a migraine week. Preference beats a trend blend. If rosemary is fashionable and it makes me feel like a kitchen I cannot leave, I pick something quieter.

The body is in the chair even when the work is abstract. Screens dry the face and narrow the field. A smell break is not skincare and it is not therapy. It is a boundary I can run when I will not run a walk. Some days I still fail and stay in the tab. The ritual is still there the next afternoon.

I wrote about [sensory load as a system resource](/p/sensory-load-is-a-system-resource/). This note is the desk version. Tech will not smell for you. You can use a low-tech cue without turning it into a stack of wearables. Two minutes, one channel that is not a screen, then back to the work or out of it. That is the whole loop break. If it becomes another thing to optimize, I have already lost.
