Layering oils is not emptying every bottle because you paid for them. A room has a volume. People in it have limits. I treat this like occupancy, not like a playlist where more tracks mean a better set.

Count who is breathing the air. You, a roommate, a cat, a neighbor on the other side of a thin wall. Ventilation next: window, door, HVAC that just recirculates. A sealed Auckland studio in July is a small box. A Louisiana room with the AC blasting is a different small box. The same four drops will feel like a spill in both if you stack citrus on mint on a resin because a chart said they "harmonize." One dominant note. A second oil only if it is quieter and you already know it. A third is how I have given myself a headache I then blamed on the sprint.

Add across days, not across one fill. Live with the lead oil. Then try a drop of something else in a later session, same room, same runtime. Write it down. If the room smells like a shop, you have already lost. Coworking is almost never the place. You do not know who has a migraine or asthma. Home offices you do not fully own still need a question asked out loud.

Device placement is part of layering. Corner by a closed curtain concentrates. Near a door shares whether people wanted it or not. Timer on. I use thirty minutes and then air. Pets, kids, pregnancy: shrink the load or switch to a personal inhaler. Dilution language is for skin; diffusion still has a dose. More oils are not synergy. They are mixed solvents in a cube.

I want a room I can stay in. That is the success condition. Not a complex blend I could describe in a caption. If you cannot name the lead note, you have layered past the point of a cue. Turn it off, open the window, try again tomorrow with one bottle.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
