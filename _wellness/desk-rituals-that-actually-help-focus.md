Focus gadgets pile up. A second monitor arm, a new lamp, an app that grades my morning. The thing that actually starts my work is smaller: same mug, two stretches I can do between the chair and the wall, same short scent. Repeatable cues beat hardware because ADHD will not negotiate with a new object every Monday. The brain wants a door. I build a boring door.

I run the sequence in order so I do not decide it. Water in the mug. Shoulders and wrists, two minutes, not a workout I will skip. Diffuser or inhaler on a timer if the room is mine. Then the calendar block, not the inbox as a warm-up. Inbox is how the ritual dies. The scent is a cue, not a treatment. If I skip the stretches and only mist the desk, I have turned the oil into theater. If I skip the oil and keep the rest, the door still works. That is how I know which part is load-bearing.

Shutdown is a second ritual, not a mirror of the first. Different note or no note. Laptop lid. Slack status. The same playlist ending, if music is part of it, so I do not drift into a third hour of "just this review." I measure this by whether I can ship at a pace I can stand next month, not by whether the morning felt cinematic. Vibe is a weak metric. A week of started blocks is a better one.

Travel breaks the props. Hotel desk, Louisiana humidity, an Auckland rental with one chair. I keep a version that fits in a pouch: mug optional, stretch still possible, personal inhaler instead of a device I will not unpack. The ritual is the sequence, not the ceramic. Shared offices lose the diffuser. They keep the stretches and the mug.

I do not add a wearable that buzzes me into focus. I do not buy a new bottle when the week is hard. Hard weeks need sleep and a walk more than a fourth cue. Pregnancy, pets, and scent-sensitive coworkers still constrain the smell part. The rest of the door is free. Build the two minutes you will run when you are already late. Then stop improving it.
