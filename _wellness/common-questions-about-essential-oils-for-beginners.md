The first questions I get are almost never about botany. They are about whether the bottle is real, how not to burn skin, and whether the cat will be fine. Fair.

Quality is not a gold sticker. I look for the botanical name (Lavandula angustifolia, not just "lavender"), a batch or lot, and a supplier who can talk about testing. GC/MS reports are useful when they exist. Price is a weak signal. Cheap oil can be extended or just old. Expensive oil can still be a fragrance mix. If the label will not tell you the plant, I do not put it on my face.

Dilution is math, not intuition. For adult topical use I treat roughly 1 percent on the face and 2 to 3 percent on the body as a starting range unless a trained person says otherwise for a short, specific job. That means drops in a measured carrier, not "a few" in the palm. Patch-test on a small area. More oil is not more results. It is more irritation. Diffusion is a different job: fewer drops, shorter sessions, air you can leave.

Natural is not safe. Poison ivy is natural. Essential oils are concentrated. Kids need lower exposure and often none on skin. Many oils are a problem for cats; some are a problem for dogs. I do not diffuse in a closed room with a cat as a lifestyle. Pregnancy and nursing change the list again. When I am unsure, I skip the bottle and keep the humidifier.

Do not drink the oils. I will repeat that because social posts still treat a glass of water plus a drop as a hack. Ingestion is not a beginner move and it is not a home protocol I use. Inhalation and diluted topical use are the paths that belong on a desk. If someone wants you to ingest for a diagnosis they made in a DMs, walk away.

I keep a small shelf on purpose. One or two oils I already tolerate, a carrier, a notebook. The rest of beginner anxiety is shopping. You do not need a starter kit of twelve. You need a room you can ventilate, a timer, and the humility to turn it off when your head says stop.
