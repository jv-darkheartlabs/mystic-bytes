Earl Grey taught me bergamot before any bottle did. The tea-adjacent calm is real for me as a scent: bright, slightly floral citrus, useful when the afternoon stacks. I diffuse a short burst indoors with a window cracked. I do not wear it to the shop in the sun.

Regular bergamot can be phototoxic on skin. Bergapten is the usual culprit. If you know the difference, FCF (furanocoumarin-free) has that fraction reduced, which lowers the skin-plus-sun risk. It does not make neat application smart, and it does not make UV optional. I still prefer inhalation. Dilute if it touches skin. Cover it. Skip the sunny commute.

This is not medical advice. Bergamot oil may help some people feel less clenched. It does not treat anxiety and it does not replace a walk, water, or a calendar with edges. Keep pets out of the mist. Store away from heat. Pregnant, nursing, or managing a condition: ask a practitioner.

Stress blends love this note because it smells like a pause. The pause still has to be actual. I pair a short session with standing up, not with another hour of sitting in citrus and calling it care.
