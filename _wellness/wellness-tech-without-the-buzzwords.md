I will wear a sleep tracker if the number changes what I do the next night. I will not wear one to collect a readiness score I argue with in the kitchen. Score theater is a dashboard for anxiety. It looks like data. It behaves like a slot machine.

Filter the rest like dependencies. Maintenance cost, failure mode, evidence that is not a keynote. A ring that wants a subscription and a perfect wear time is a product. A cheap notebook that says "asleep after 1, wrecked at standup" is also data. I keep the tool that moves bedtime, caffeine cutoff, or the walk I keep skipping. I delete the tool that grades a Tuesday I cannot redo. ADHD will chase a streak. I do not need a vendor designing that loop.

Scent gadgets get the same pass. A ceramic diffuser I can wash, a timer I can see, one oil I tolerate: stay. An app-controlled orb with "mood algorithms" and a cartridge club: go. Calendar boundaries still do more for load than a device that mists when Slack spikes. Integrate with the calendar. Do not replace the calendar. A humid night and a dry winter office both wreck sleep. Neither required a recovery score to tell me I should have closed the laptop.

Buzzwords are the tell. Optimization, biohacking, closed-loop nervous system, a wellness OS. If the sentence could ship as a Series A deck, I put the watch back in the drawer. Complementary maintenance is smaller: light in the morning if I can get it, food that is not only coffee, movement, a clinician when the chart needs one. Tech can count hours if counting changes the hours. It cannot mint health by graphing it.

I am not anti-gadget. I like a quiet motor and a battery I understand. I am anti-theater that charges overnight so it can insult me in color. If the score does not change behavior in two weeks, it is decoration. Take it off. Keep the bedtime you already knew you needed.
