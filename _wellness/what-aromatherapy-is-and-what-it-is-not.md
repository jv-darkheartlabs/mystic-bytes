Aromatherapy is a method. You take plant-derived essential oils and you use them mainly by smell, sometimes by diluted contact with skin, as a complement to the rest of your care. That sentence is the shelf. Everything else is marketing trying to climb off it.

I keep the definition tight because the word gets rented by people selling cures. Inhalation is the honest path for most of what I do at a desk: a diffuser on a timer, a tissue, a personal inhaler, a room that does not belong to a cat. Diluted topical use is a second path, with math and a patch test, not a neat oil rolled on a pulse point because a video said so. Ingesting oils is not a home habit I recommend. Swallowing a concentrated plant extract is not the same job as smelling it. If anyone tells you to drink a bottle for a diagnosis they just made, that is not aromatherapy. That is a problem.

Complementary means alongside. Sleep still matters. Food still matters. A clinician still owns diagnosis, prescriptions, and emergencies. I use scent for rituals, sensory boundaries, and comfort I can observe: I started the work block, I left the work block, the room feels less like a server closet. I do not use it to name a disease or to taper a medication. Psychiatry, dermatology, and urgent care are not competitors I am trying to beat with a citrus note.

The shelf thesis is how I shop and how I write these notes. If a claim fits on the shelf (this smell is a cue, this diluted oil is tolerable on my skin, this session lasted thirty minutes), I will test it. If a claim needs a white coat and a lab, it does not belong on my desk. Quality still matters on the shelf: botanical name, batch, a supplier you can ask questions of. "Natural" is not a safety certificate. Kids, pets, and pregnancy change the room. Many oils are a bad idea around cats. Diffusion is shared air.

I came to this as a builder with ADHD and too many screens, not as someone looking for a new religion. The body still has to sit in the chair. Aromatherapy is one small tool on that shelf. It is not the wall.
