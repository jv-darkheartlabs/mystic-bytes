On a ferry I would rather smell ginger than swallow a story about essential oils as medicine. Ginger as food is the better-studied path. The essential oil is a backup scent, not a drug, and nothing here replaces medical care.

Inhalation is how I use it for motion: a diluted personal inhaler, or a drop on a tissue held away from the eyes, before the crossing gets choppy. I do not put neat oil on inner wrists and then grip a rail. Ginger can irritate skin. If it meets skin at all, it goes into a carrier first, small area, patch-tested on a day I am not already nauseated. Photosensitivity is not the main issue here. Heat and rash are.

It may help some people with mild motion discomfort. It does not treat pregnancy nausea, chemotherapy nausea, vestibular disorders, or a bug you picked up in transit. Those belong to a clinician. Chronic or severe nausea is not a blending project.

Do not ingest the essential oil. Keep it away from pets, heat, and children. If you are pregnant, nursing, or managing a condition, follow medical advice, not a travel note. I would rather eat crystallized ginger and look at the horizon than pretend a bottle is a prescription.
