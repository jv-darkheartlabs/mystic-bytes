Cinnamon bark oil is a hot oil. Treat it like one. The smell is bakery-adjacent until it hits skin, and then it is irritation waiting to happen. I do not use it neat. I barely use it at all.

When I do, it is winter, a large room, and a low drop count in a diffuser. One drop. Maybe two if the space is big and the window is open. This is not daily perfume and it is not a desk companion for a closed office. Mucous membranes, children, and pets do not get a vote once the mist is in the air, so I keep sessions short and the bottle capped.

Cinnamon bark may add a warm note to a cold-weather evening. It does not treat illness. It can inflame skin, eyes, and airways. If it ever meets skin, dilute it heavily in a carrier and patch-test first. Keep it off sun-exposed skin; photosensitivity is a real risk here, not a footnote. Do not ingest it.

Store it away from heat. If you are pregnant, nursing, or managing a chronic condition, skip this bottle unless a qualified practitioner says otherwise. I would rather smell nothing than pretend a spice oil is harmless because the candle version seemed festive.
