Every year someone ships a bracelet that mists oil when your heart rate ticks up. I have never wanted another device that needs charging in order to smell something. Mood LEDs that pulse "calm" are a UI for a feeling you already have or do not. They do not make the room kinder. They make a charging cable.

Useful signal is boring. A diffuser with a quiet motor and a timer I can see. One oil I already like, not a cartridge subscription that guesses my mood from a quiz. I set thirty minutes when I start a block or when I shut the lid. The gadget is a clock. The scent is the cue. If I have to open an app, create an account, and grant location to release two drops of "focus blend," the stack has already failed the maintenance test.

Wearables that puff lavender exist. Ask what problem they solve that a tissue and a bottle do not. If the answer is "the algorithm knows when you are stressed," I am out. Heart rate is not consent to scent the air around other people, and it is not a diagnosis. I also do not want a necklace leaking oil onto a shirt I have to wear to a meeting. Dilution still applies when the device is cute. Pets still apply when the device is automatic. An unattended mister in a small room is how you get a headache and a cat you should not have stressed.

I filter this the way I filter desk toys. Maintenance cost, failure mode, whether I will use it on a bad Wednesday. A ceramic diffuser I can wash beats a Bluetooth orb with a firmware page. If the useful signal is "time passed," a kitchen timer and an open window will do. Dry processed air made me care about the room. It did not require an LED ring.

Hype will keep shipping. I will keep a short list: timer, oil I tolerate, room I control. Complementary, not a treatment. If a product needs a keynote to explain why you should smell it, I do not need it on the desk.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
