Thyme oil has a reputation it did not earn as a household ritual. Phenol-rich types, especially thymol-heavy ones, are strong. Respect that. A kitchen herb on the board is not the same as a bottle in a diffuser, and neither is a registered disinfectant.

I use it rarely, in a ventilated room, for a few minutes. High concentration can irritate lungs and skin. I do not wipe counters with it. I do not "clean the air" with it during cold season and call that a protocol. If a surface needs disinfection, I use a product made for that job.

This is not medical advice. Thyme oil may smell medicinal and focused in a large, aired space. It does not cure infection. Dilute heavily if it touches skin, which I usually avoid. Keep pets far from the plume; birds and cats have no buffer for this kind of intensity. Store away from heat. Pregnant, nursing, or managing a condition: ask a practitioner before you even uncap it.

Read the chemotype on the label if the seller lists one. Then still start smaller than you think. Reputation is marketing. Dose is the part you control.
