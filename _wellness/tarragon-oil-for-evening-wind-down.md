Tarragon smells green and faintly of anise, closer to a kitchen than a candy shop. I like that in the evening if I keep the dose small. Push it and you get liquorice overdose: sweet, loud, and stuck in the room after you wanted quiet.

One drop in a diffuser is plenty. I treat it as a wind-down mark after the laptop closes, not as a night-long fog. Polarizing is the honest word. If you hate fennel or tarragon on food, you will hate this in air. Trust that.

This is not medical advice. Tarragon oil may suit a short evening ritual. It does not treat insomnia and it does not replace dimmer light. Dilute for skin and patch-test if you insist on touching it; I usually do not. Keep pets out of the mist. Store away from heat. Never ingest the bottle because the herb is edible. Those are different products.

Pregnant, nursing, or managing a condition: ask a practitioner. Small dose is the whole practice. If the anise note turns heavy, open the window and stop. I would rather under-scent the room and still want tarragon next week.
