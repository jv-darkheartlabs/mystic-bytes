Motion and a sour stomach are logistics problems before they are scent problems. Fennel oil shows up in travel kits because the smell is licorice-green and some people associate it with milder digestive discomfort. That association is not a diagnosis, and this is not medical advice.

I do not ingest fennel essential oil. Food fennel and tea are a different conversation. The bottled oil is concentrated. If I use it at all on the road, it is a brief inhalation from a diluted wipe or a tiny amount in a personal inhaler, not a drop on the tongue and not a bottle waving through a cabin. Skin contact means a carrier oil first. Undiluted fennel on the abdomen is how you get a rash and still feel awful.

It may help some people feel settled enough to eat a plain meal after a bumpy transfer. It does not treat food poisoning, ulcers, or anything that needs a clinician. Persistent pain, fever, or vomiting is a medical problem. Smell is not.

Keep it away from pets, heat, and children. If you are pregnant, nursing, estrogen-sensitive, or managing a chronic condition, skip this one unless someone qualified says otherwise. Dilute. Cap the bottle. Do not make a stomach complaint wait on a ritual.
