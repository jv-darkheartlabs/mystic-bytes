I used to land and unpack serums like I was setting up a lab. The face wanted water and sleep. Recycled cabin air dries you out. Hotel water is a different hardness than home. The bathroom lighting makes every decision look urgent. None of that is a reason to run twelve steps you will abandon at 1 a.m. local.

The ritual I actually keep is short. Gentle cleanse to get the plane off the skin. Moisturizer I already know, enough to feel occlusive without a sting. Water in a glass, not only a mist bottle I will lose in the toiletry bag. Then the lights go down. If I have a diluted facial oil I tolerate, it can go on after the cream as comfort, not as treatment for puffiness I have diagnosed in a mirror. Massage is optional. I skip it when I am angry at the pillow. Irritated skin gets less, not a new acid because duty-free was bored.

Hotel water can leave a film or strip you, depending on the building. I do not fight it with a travel-sized retinoid I only use when I remember. Consistency at home beats a heroic night in a strange bathroom. SPF in the morning if I will see daylight through the lobby or a window seat the next day. Aotearoa sun after a long-haul from the States is not a Louisiana porch you already understand. Hats if I walk it off. The face is part of landing, not a separate project.

I leave the twelve-step kit at home. Actives that need patience do not belong in a week of time zones. Essential oils stay diluted and off any skin that is already tight or broken. Pets are not in the hotel room, but pregnancy and a wrecked barrier still are, if those are your constraints. This is not medical care for a rash you picked up in transit. If something is swelling, painful, or spreading, that is a clinic, not a rollerball.

The point of a recovery ritual is that you will do it when you are stupid with fatigue. Cleanse, moisturize, water, sleep. I can run that in New Orleans or in Tāmaki Makaurau without a packing cube dedicated to hope. If I add scent, it is one familiar note that means the day is over, not a new blend I invented at the gate.
