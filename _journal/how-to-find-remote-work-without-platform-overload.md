Twelve job boards is a hobby with worse lighting. I keep two, and I treat the rest as noise that wants my mornings. One generalist board I actually check. One community or niche channel where the same people post more than once a year. If I cannot name both without opening a bookmark folder, I have too many.

Overload looks like productivity. Tabs, alerts, a spreadsheet of "applied." The listings go stale while you are still formatting the same paragraph. I lost weeks that way before I admitted the problem was not effort. It was the feed.

A search rhythm is a small ritual. Same two places, same weekday, a timer so it cannot eat the afternoon. Filters that match how I live: remote that is real, hours that can meet Oceania, stack I can defend in an interview. "Remote worldwide" with a daily stand-up at 2am NZST is an office with a nicer adjective. I skip it.

Portfolio beats volume. One repo with a README that tells you how to run it. One piece of writing that shows how I explain a tradeoff. One sentence on async: I write things down, I do not vanish, I will not pretend a beach is a status update. That packet travels further than twenty identical cover notes.

This is not the same work as tailoring a single application to a single team. That essay is about fit. This one is about not drowning in inventory. You can tailor into the void if the void is twelve platforms. Cut the inventory first. Then spend the recovered hours on the two listings that survived the filter.

Remote search is a slow pipeline. Treat it like a job with office hours, not a weekend binge that leaves you raw for Monday's actual work. I learned that between Louisiana job boards and Auckland mornings, when the hemisphere made "I'll just check one more site" into a second shift. Two platforms. Then close the laptop and go build something you would be willing to show.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
