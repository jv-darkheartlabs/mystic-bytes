Capsule culture wants a suitcase that looks like a mood board. I want seven days of laundry and a zipper that still closes after I add the jacket I actually wear. Light packing is a wash cycle, not a personality. If I cannot get clothes clean once a week, I packed a costume change, not a life.

I count in loads. Seven shirts that dry overnight. Two trousers that survive a desk and a wet footpath. Underwear and socks for the full week, because running out is how you buy junk you will abandon. One pair of shoes that can walk Tāmaki Makaurau hills and sit through a call without announcing themselves. A compact rain shell, because Wellington does not care about your outfit theory. Merino if I can afford it: wear twice, wash once, hang on a hanger that came with the room.

The rest is vanity weight. A fourth pair of shoes. The jacket that only works in a photograph. Specialty pieces I will not wear because the week is meetings and grocery runs. I have shipped that extra layer home from Auckland more than once. The box cost more than the honesty would have.

"Living large" was the old lie in this title. A bigger wardrobe does not make a city larger. It makes Monday heavier. I left Louisiana knowing how to dress for humidity: breathable fabric, nothing precious, accept that you will sweat and then you will rinse. Aotearoa taught me the inverse week. Wet cold that never quite dries if you packed cotton and optimism. The rule held in both places. If it cannot survive a sink or a laundromat, it does not belong in the bag.

Work kit is a different note. This one is cloth. I do not negotiate laptop chargers against socks. I negotiate shirts against the building's washing machine, and I ask about that machine before I book a month. No laundry on site means I need a neighborhood place with hours that match my sleep, or I need fewer clothes and a stricter rinse.

Pack, wear the bag around the block, unpack what you did not reach for. Do it before the airport. Future-you at a baggage scale is not a muse. She is a person who still has to do laundry on Thursday.
