Fluency theater is a phrasebook you flash at dinner and abandon by dessert. Courtesy is the five sentences you will actually say at a counter when you are tired. I study those first. Hello. Please. Thank you. Excuse me. The numbers I need for money and time. How to ask someone to repeat themselves without making it their problem.

In Aotearoa that starts with te reo Māori I can hold in my mouth without turning it into a performance. Kia ora. Thank you. Place names said as they are written, not as a US mouth wants them. I still mis-hit vowels. I still try. The try is the point, and it is cheaper than a speech about how much I respect the culture.

Apps belong in another note. This one is human effort. I listen to how cashiers close a sale. I copy the rhythm more than the vocabulary. In New Orleans I grew up knowing that how you greet someone is part of the transaction. That habit travels if I let it. It does not require me to pretend I am fluent in a week.

What does not help: reciting a paragraph I memorized for a video. Correcting a local who switched to English to be kind. Asking for a language lesson from someone who is trying to finish a shift. I have done the last one by accident. The face tells you. You apologize and you buy the thing and you leave.

I keep a tiny card in the wallet with the phrases for a pharmacy, a lost bag, and a landlord. Handwritten, because the act of writing is the study. If I need a menu or a street sign, I use a camera. If I need to be a person in a room, I start with the greeting I practiced on the plane. The room gets to decide how much English comes back. My job is not to win. It is to show I came in as a guest.

Effort compounds on the third visit to the same dairy. They stop bracing. That is the enrichment. Not a certificate. Not a streak in an app I will ignore after the first fortnight.
