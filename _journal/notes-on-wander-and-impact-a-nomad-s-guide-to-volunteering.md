If the first question is how it will look in a post, stay home. Useful work is quieter than that. I look for organisations that already exist, that asked for help in public, and that can name a task. A website that has been broken for a year. A Saturday packing line. Docs nobody wanted to write. Skills I actually have, offered for a stretch I can finish before I change cities.

Volunteer tourism is a product. It sells you a feeling and sells the host a stream of untrained guests. I have watched groups photograph children as proof of goodness. I will not do that, and I will not stand next to it for a caption. If an org wants pictures, they will say so, and they will own the camera. My phone stays in a pocket.

The useful test is duration. One afternoon of "helping" at a marae or a food pantry because it is on a blog list is extraction with extra steps. Recurring hours, even two, let someone decide whether you are worth the briefing. In Tāmaki Makaurau I would rather be the boring person who shows up than the visiting expert who leaves a mess in the CMS. Louisiana church basements taught me the same rule. The people who count are the ones who wash the trays.

Ask what they need. Then shut up long enough to hear the answer. They may not need your stack. They may need someone who can lift, or who can sit with a spreadsheet without turning it into a redesign. I have been most useful when I was least impressive.

Money is allowed. A donation without a tour can be the honest version of help when your stay is short. Time is allowed when you will still be here next Thursday. What I refuse is the sandwich of service and personal brand. The work either needed doing or it did not. My archive does not get a vote.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
