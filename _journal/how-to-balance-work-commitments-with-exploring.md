I cancelled a ferry because a deploy was still red at 4pm. The harbour would have been beautiful. The rollback was the job. I walked to the dairy for milk instead and did not call it a failure of curiosity. Tourism is not a second job. If I treat every city like a checklist, the work becomes the thing I resent, and then I do both badly.

The protocol that survived is small. One protected explore block per stay. Not a daily itinerary. Not a dawn hike before standup. A block on the calendar with the same dignity as a review, usually a weekday afternoon or a Saturday that does not sit on a release. I pick one thing I will actually remember: a ridgeline, a museum, a neighborhood I can walk without a map. If the stay is two weeks, that is one block. If the stay is a month, it might be two. More than that and I start stealing from sleep.

When the sprint is on fire, I cancel the tourism. I say this out loud because otherwise guilt fills the gap. The mountain will be there. The incident will not wait. I have sat on a bus to a lookout while Slack filled with pages I should have been on. The photos were fine. The week after was not.

Exploring also fails when I use it as a reward I never collect. If the only outdoor time is "after I finish," I never finish. The protected block has to be real on a quiet week too, or the rule is just punishment. The point is a ceiling and a floor: you get to leave the desk, and you do not owe the city a performance.

Async work is what makes this possible from Aotearoa. Overlap hours stay overlap hours. I do not move a client call to fit a waterfall. I move the waterfall. Louisiana taught me a long walk can still count as work if the writing happened. It did not teach me to disappear for three days and call it research.

I keep a note at the top of the stay: what shipped, what I saw, what I skipped. The skipped column is allowed to be long. A city I barely toured and a week I closed beats a full camera roll and a half-written spec. The ship goes first. Then, if the board is green, I go look at the water.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
