I have watched myself spend a workday hunting a charging cable, a booking confirmation, and the one PDF a border desk will actually accept. Skip the night-before list and the move becomes the whole week: unpaid operations wearing a boarding pass.

I run a move-day checklist the night before, on paper, in the pouch I can grab without thinking. Passport, visas or eTA printouts, insurance card, accommodation address written down in case the phone dies, chargers, meds, the adapter that matches this country's sockets. The list is ugly. Ugly lists get followed. Beautiful systems get abandoned in an airport queue.

The document pouch is physical on purpose. Cloud copies matter. So does a folder you can hand to yourself at 5 a.m. when the Wi-Fi in the departure hall is a rumor and your 2FA app wants a signal it does not have. I keep scans offline on the laptop as well. Redundancy is not drama. It is how you avoid becoming the person arguing with a gate agent while a standup starts without you.

Postal forwarding and transit planning are their own notes. This is narrower: the hours around leaving one bed and occupying the next. Checkout time, luggage storage, the gap between landing and keys, the SIM that actually works before you need a rideshare. If those pieces are not named, they eat the first workday, then the second.

I give logistics one hour on ordinary days. Calendar it. Bank, bookings, "where is the pouch," nothing else. If logistics wants the whole afternoon, the system is too heavy or the trip is too ambitious. Shrink the hop. Do not promote yourself to full-time travel ops while still pretending you have a shipping calendar.

The point of moving well is to stop thinking about moving. Get the pouch right, run the list, take the hour, then close the tab. Work needs the rest of the day.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
