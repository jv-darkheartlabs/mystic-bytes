The second Tuesday at the same café, the barista started making the usual without a speech. That was more community than a rooftop mixer with lanyards. I do not need a scene. I need a place I return to until a human face is not a first-time transaction. Repeat visits are the whole method.

Skill swaps beat small talk. I have traded a debugging hour for a map of a neighborhood's quiet desks. I have read someone's README in exchange for a frank warning about a building's upload. That is a relationship with a job attached. Networking events that sell belonging as a product tend to sell workshops and group chats that go quiet when the organizer's lease ends. I have paid for one of those. I left with a tote and no one to text when the internet died.

Coworking gets the same second-visit rule, but I do not confuse a hot desk with a friend group. The tribe-and-mixer notes live elsewhere. This one is narrower: pick one or two rooms and inhabit them on a schedule. Same morning if you can. People who work at the same hour start to nod. Nodding is not intimacy. It is enough to ask where the printer is, and sometimes enough to get a dinner that is not a performance.

What I avoid: "founders dinners" that are pitches, WhatsApp groups named after a lifestyle, anyone who needs you to be inspiring in public. What I keep: the person who also writes in the corner on Thursdays, the librarian who remembers the card, the one DM that stays useful after I change cities. Repeatable means it survives a flight. A scene usually does not. It needs a postcode and a brand.

Louisiana social life can be loud and sudden. Aotearoa can be reserved until you have shown up enough times to be boring. I like the boring version for work. You can still go dancing. You do not have to build your professional life out of strangers who will forget you when the season turns.

If the second Tuesday still feels like a hotel lobby, change the café. Do not add a third event. Depth before volume. The calendar already has overlap hours. Community that demands a fourth night out is another job. I already have one.
