Charm is not a strategy. I have watched people perform likability at a host and still get a Wi-Fi story that changes after the first week. I ask for the monthly price in writing. I ask what is included. I ask what happens if the connection fails or the water heater dies. Then I decide. Walking away has saved me more money than a discount I could not enforce.

Weekly listings pretend to be flexible. Monthly is a different conversation. I wait until I know I can stay, then I write: dates, rate, deposit, cancellation, internet, desk, quiet hours. I do not do this as a speech in a doorway. I send it. I want their reply in the same thread I can find at 11pm when something is wrong.

I do not haggle for sport. If the rate is fair and the room works, I pay it. If the rate is tourist-week pricing for a month of actual living, I ask for the monthly number once. If they cannot do it, I leave. There is always another listing. There is not always another month of my attention.

Deposits and cleaning fees need dates and conditions. I photograph the room on arrival. Unromantic. Useful. I do not sign a house-rule PDF that bans visitors and also claims to be a home, then argue about it later. I either accept the rules or I do not book.

Tax and the builder-budget note live elsewhere. This is only the stay: the rate, the terms, the exit. Be polite. Be brief. Do not owe them a personality. A host who needs to be charmed to honour a written rate is a host I will not work under.

If they will not put it in writing, there is no deal. There is a vibe. I do not work from vibes.
