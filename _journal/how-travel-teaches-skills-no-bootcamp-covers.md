Bootcamps teach you to pass interviews. They do not teach you to sit at a counter while two clerks disagree about a form and still keep your voice even. That patience is a skill. I did not learn it in a sprint. I learned it in offices that close at 4pm, in queues that do not care about my standup, in the long minute after someone says "come back with a certified copy."

Every country has its own interface for bureaucracy. Fields that look optional are not. References who must live in-country. Proof of funds dated within thirty days. You learn to read the form twice, then read the PDF that contradicts the website. You screenshot the version you submitted. Politeness is not a performance. It is how you keep the conversation from becoming a wall.

Under stress, systems show their seams. A train app that dies in a tunnel. A bank that flags a card because you used it in Auckland after New Orleans. A laptop that will not boot in a room with one working outlet. You rebuild from backup. You call from a number that is not yours. You wait. Adaptation is the ability to change the plan without narrating your suffering to the room.

Currency math becomes muscle. A second clock lives in your head, and you still do not steal from sleep to look flexible. I keep a running doc: what worked, what failed, what to pack next time, which office actually answers email. Consumption without notes is just airports. Notes without curiosity are a spreadsheet. I want the education.

Louisiana taught me to talk to people without treating them like scenery. Aotearoa taught me that preparation is a form of respect. Neither is in a bootcamp curriculum. Both show up when the work is remote and the desk keeps moving.

I treat this as continuing education. I do not call it a gap year, and I do not wait for inspiration to file the next form.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
