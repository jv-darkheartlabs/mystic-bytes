Spray-and-pray applications feel like work because they fill a spreadsheet. They do not fill a stack. I wasted a season sending the same paragraph to companies whose hours would have wrecked sleep in Aotearoa. The rejections were not mysterious. I had applied to a calendar I could not keep.

Tailored means you read the role until you can name the actual work. The language, the domain, the shape of the team. Then you write to that, not to a template with the company name swapped. One repo they can clone. One sentence on how you handle async. One honest map of your hours. If their stand-up is 2am in Tāmaki Makaurau and they will not move it, you are not a fit. Say so before they spend a panel on you.

Timezone honesty is the part people skip because it feels like shrinking the market. It shrinks the market that would have burned you. Remote worldwide often means the office stayed in one city and the adjectives traveled. I ask where the overlapping hours are, and I believe the calendar more than the careers page.

This is a different problem from platform overload. Overload is too many tabs. Fit is whether the work matches what you actually build, and whether the clock matches a body that has to live in Oceania for a while. You can have two platforms and still spray. You can have one well-written note to a team that uses your tools and get further.

I keep a short list of what I will not fake. Stack I have shipped in. Hours I can protect. Communication that is mostly writing. If a listing wants daily video from a timezone I cannot sustain, I skip it. Louisiana to Auckland already spends enough of my sleep on the hemisphere. I will not donate the rest to a job that needed someone in Brooklyn.

Apply fewer places. Name the work. Name the clock. Let the rest of the board make noise without you.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
