I do not pick the next city from a mood board. I pick it from four questions I resent and still use. Can I enter and stay legally for the weeks I need. Can I overlap with the people I work with without destroying sleep. Can I afford the real daily cost, not the hostel headline. Can I ship from the pipe in that neighborhood at the hours I actually work.

Visa first, always. A beautiful month you cannot lawfully occupy is a story for someone else. I read the activity rules, not the tourism slogan. Remote work is often the clause that is fuzzy, and fuzzy is where people get surprised. I am not going to pretend I enjoy immigration PDFs. I will pretend I like being able to leave through the same airport I entered.

Overlap hours are the second filter because Oceania does not negotiate with a US calendar. From Tāmaki Makaurau, some "quick calls" land in the wrong part of my night. If the team is real, I choose bases that make a humane overlap possible, or I agree in writing that async is the default and live with that. A city that photographs well at dusk is a bad trade for six weeks of 1 a.m. standups.

Cost is daily burn, which I treat as its own discipline elsewhere. Here it is only a veto: if the city is a luxury relative to the work, I do not go "for the experience" and then panic. Internet is a veto too. I want evidence beyond a listing's "high-speed" adjective: recent tests, a backup SIM, a coworking option I would actually use if the apartment fails.

Louisiana is not in this matrix as romance. It is the origin I measure against: humidity I already understand, bureaucracy I can smell coming. Aotearoa is in it as a real candidate, not as a screensaver. The next place has to survive the week, not the feed.

If two cities tie, I pick the one that fails more softly: easier visa exit, cheaper mistake, better failover. That is not timid. That is how you still have a runway after the pretty one would have wrecked you.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
