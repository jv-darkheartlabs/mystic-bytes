By week three the café is just a café. The view has been used up. The barista knows the order and you know which table gets the afternoon sun in your eyes. This is the real test. Inspiration packed its bag around day twelve. What remains is whether you have a sequence that survives boredom.

I keep the same first task. Not the same outcome, the same door. Inbox to a defined zero, or the hardest ticket before messages, or a page of notes before code. The door has to be small enough that a grey morning in Wellington still fits through it. If I wait to feel like a person who lives here, I will wait until the lease ends.

Week one was about sitting down at all. This week is about not inventing a new life every Monday. I do not hunt a better coworking loft because the current one has a noisy espresso machine. I put on headphones or I change hours. I do not replan the city because the novelty debt came due. Errands go on a list for after the block, the way they did at home, even if home was a humidity that felt like a wet coat.

Systems I actually keep: a wake window that only shifts for real timezone work, not for nightlife I am not attending. A weekly grocery that is not a treasure hunt. One social thing on the calendar so the silence does not turn into a story about failure. The rest is repetition. Louisiana summers taught me that heat makes you skip the walk and then skip the work. Aotearoa taught me that drizzle does the same if you let weather become a personality. I dress and I go.

When motivation is gone I do not read about habits. I start the timer for twenty-five minutes and I accept an ugly first sentence. The spark was never the job. The job is the same in a new postcode as it was in the old one, except the chairs are different and the birds are louder. Week three is when you find out if you came to work or to collect rooms.
