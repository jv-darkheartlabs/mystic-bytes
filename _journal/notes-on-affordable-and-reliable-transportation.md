I default to transit. A rental car is a last resort, not a personality. In Tāmaki Makaurau the AT HOP card has done more for my budget than any pitch about a compact automatic and "freedom." Buses, trains, and the ferries to Devonport or Waiheke cover more of a working week than people who land and walk straight to the rental desk want to admit.

The ferry is a commute with a harbour attached. I have written on the Devonport run when the cabin was quiet and a deadline did not care about the view. I have also missed a sailing and learned that the next one is the plan, not a catastrophe. Build slack into the morning. Do not schedule a client call for the minute you think you will dock.

Airports as offices are a last resort. I have done it. The lighting is hostile, the outlets are a hunt, and someone will sit down with a phone on speaker. If a connection is long enough to work, I look for a lounge day pass only when the work cannot wait. Otherwise I read, walk, and protect the battery. A gate is not a desk.

Wellington's buses in the wind taught me a rain shell is part of the commute. I do not rent a car to "see more" and then spend the day parking, paying, and driving on the left while my brain is still on Louisiana time. When I need a car, I hire it for a specific day with a specific job: a trailhead, a supermarket in a place with no bus, a pickup I cannot make otherwise. Then I return it.

Affordable and reliable are not the same as cheapest. A cheap trip that dumps you in the rain two kilometres from the door is not cheaper than a slightly dearer ride that lands you dry and on time. I keep a backup: a rideshare app logged in, a little cash, the local taxi number written down in case the phone dies.

Walk when the city is walkable. Auckland's sprawl is real. I do not pretend Ponsonby and the airport are a pleasant stroll. I plan the first week around AT and my feet. The car can wait until the work has a reason.
