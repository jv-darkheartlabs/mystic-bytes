The 48-hour note is not a pitch. It is a receipt. You were the person who mentioned the flaky CI on Friday. I thought about the workaround you described. Here is a link, or a thank you, or nothing but the memory. No deck. No "let's find time."

I send it while I still remember their face. After two days the conversation turns into a vibe, and vibes produce messages that sound like everyone else's. Specific is the whole trick. A talk slide. A library they named. The fact they also hate circulating. If I cannot name a detail, I do not write. Silence is better than a template.

Keep it short. Three to six sentences. Who I am in one line if they met a dozen people. The detail. An optional offer that does not create work for them: a gist, a doc, a "no need to reply." The pest version is a thread, a calendar link, a PDF of things I have built, a request for intros to people they mentioned once while holding a beer.

If they reply, I match their temperature. A short answer gets a short answer. A real question gets a real answer. I do not escalate a thank-you into a coffee unless they open that door. Travel already makes people flaky. Adding pursuit on top of a meetup is how you become the person they mute.

This is the other half of hating the room. In the room I had one conversation and left. Afterward I do the small adult thing so the conversation was not only air. I am not building a pipeline. I am closing a loop. If nothing comes of it, the note still cost less than lingering by the door.

I have sent notes from Auckland to people I met once in another city and never met again. That is fine. The point was not a harvest. Louisiana manners say you follow up when someone helped you. I kept that. I dropped the part where follow-up means a funnel. One memory. Two days. Then leave the thread alone.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
