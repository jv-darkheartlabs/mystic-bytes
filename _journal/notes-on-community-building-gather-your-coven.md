I do not need a clubhouse. I need a desk I can find without thinking and a room that does not require me to explain myself at the door. Community, for me, has been a library on Lorne Street, a café that learned the order, a gym I could walk to in the rain. Repeat spaces. The same chair on a Tuesday. That is the whole method.

A dedicated community product wants you to join, badge, and show up to mixers. I have tried a few. The ones that sold lifestyle without a quiet table were a poor fit. I am not looking for a house with a Slack channel and a brand. I am looking for a place I can return to until the staff stop treating me like a tourist and I stop treating the city like a set.

Show up enough times that your body knows the route. That is how I got a nod from a librarian and a regular's patience from a barista during a US-hours call I should have taken elsewhere. Skill swaps and coworking days have their place. They are not the same as a space that holds you when you are not performing usefulness.

When I change cities, I pick one repeat space in the first week. Not five. One. A library if the Wi-Fi holds. A council rec centre if I need to move. A hall noticeboard if that is where the city still posts what is happening. I write the address down. I go back before I go hunting for a better one.

How you keep the same people in a rhythm is a different note. This one is the architecture: rooms you re-enter until they become ordinary. Ordinary is the goal. Belonging that requires a password and a personality is a clubhouse. I will take the library.

— JV · Dark Heart Labs.
