December in Tāmaki Makaurau is peak summer. US calendars still treat it as a winding-down month. You get long evenings and a standup that lands after dinner. The mismatch is the work. Seasons here do not politely wait for anyone's Q4.

**Summer (Dec–Feb).** Auckland and Wellington fill with light. Mood lifts. Sleep gets sloppy if you chase the last of the sun after US calls. I have done the 10pm walk and the 7am review in the same stretch and paid for it the next day in slow sentences and missed nuance. Book coworking early in Tāmaki Makaurau. Power and air conditioning are not luxuries when a laptop sits on a warm desk. Melbourne in January is the same problem with better coffee. If you cannot sleep, you cannot ship. Blackout fabric beats another waterfront photograph.

**Autumn (Mar–May).** Shoulder season in Aotearoa. Fewer tourists, more seats, fibre in the city cores that actually behaves. This is when I test apartment Wi-Fi at peak hours before signing anything monthly. A quiet listing in April can still choke at 6pm when the building comes home. Wellington wind will find the gap in a cheap rain shell. I keep one extra merino in the bag. Cafés empty enough to think in, which is useful until you confuse emptiness with a social life.

**Winter (Jun–Aug).** North Island wetter. South Island colder. Café hopping only works if you can dry clothes overnight. A compact drying rack for merino is dull and it has saved more sprints than any scenic listing. Melbourne winters suit indoor-focused work if the heating is real. I have sat in a beautiful room that was a fridge after 5pm. Ask about heating before you arrive. Do not assume the listing photo was taken in July. Short days help sleep if you let them. They punish you if you keep US hours and call it flexibility.

**Spring (Sep–Nov).** Pollen for some of us. Conference season for others. Rents start to think about summer. Walk neighborhoods before December prices and decide whether a city is a two-week experiment or a month you can afford without moving the desk again.

Pick one base. Stay two weeks before you commit to a month. The first week is logistics and jet lag. The second week is whether the daylight, the clock, and the desk agree with the work. A blog cannot teach you that. The city will, if you give it enough days without already packing for the next one.
