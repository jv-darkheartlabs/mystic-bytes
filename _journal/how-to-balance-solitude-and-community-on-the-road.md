A coworking floor can sound like a blender after two hours of headphones. The espresso machine, the sales call at the next table, the door that never quite closes. I used to treat that as atmosphere. It is load. When the load is high, I do not become more social. I become worse at sentences.

Solitude and company both have to be scheduled. If I leave them to mood, I get the wrong one. After a move I default to silence because unpacking is already noise. A week later I notice I have spoken to a barista and a checkout screen. That is not a personality. That is an empty calendar.

I am closer to introvert than not. Contact still has to be booked. A walk with one person. A standing Tuesday at the same desk. A call home that is not squeezed into the gap after a late standup. If it is not on the calendar, it loses to email. Extroverts I have roomed with fail the other way. They collect dinners until the deep-work block is a rumor. They need focus hours with the same seriousness other people reserve for drinks.

Coworking twice before you expect friends. The first day you are a new badge. The second day someone might remember your name. Friendship is slower than that, and the spaces that promise community on the website often deliver hot desks and a Slack no one reads. I have left after a month with no one to text. That is a data point, not a moral failure.

Oceania distances make the quiet louder. Tāmaki Makaurau to Wellington is a flight you feel. Louisiana relatives sit in a timezone that turns a casual hello into a scheduling problem. Recurring touchpoints matter more than a packed week of strangers. One person you will see again beats five events that leave your nervous system ringing.

Track which you actually need. Loneliness and solitude can share a room. One wants a human voice. The other wants the door closed. I write it down on Sunday: two blocks of people, two blocks of silence, and a rule that headphones are not a personality. When I skip the silence, the work gets sloppy. When I skip the people, the work gets brittle. The protocol is boring. It is also the only version that has lasted past the first fortnight.
